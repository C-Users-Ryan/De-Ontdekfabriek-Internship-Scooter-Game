# Facilitator Guide: Impact Makers Around The World: Kenya

**For:** Workshop facilitators at De Ontdekfabriek and TATOE partner organisations.
**Not for:** developers (see [Start Here (README)](README.md) for the technical handover).
**Activity:** Drive Through Kenya, *Impact Makers Around The World: Kenya* (working name: Kenya Scooter Game).
**Session length:** about 10 minutes per rotation (about 2 minutes of play per student, plus hand-offs and a debrief).
**Last updated:** Sprint 8 · June 2026. This guide matches the current shipped build. The short laminated version is the [Quick Reference card](07%20Facilitator%20Quick%20Reference.md); where the two ever differ, the Quick Reference card is the daily authority.

> **Kort gezegd (voor begeleiders)**
> Je hebt geen Unity en geen technische kennis nodig. Open het spel, zet het vast met **schermvastzetten** zodat kinderen er niet uit kunnen, en laat groepjes om de beurt spelen. Een kind **tikt op een teamnaam** om te starten, **kantelt de tablet** om te sturen, en geeft na zijn beurt door aan de volgende. Wil je het spel makkelijker, rustiger of anders? Houd de **linkerbovenhoek 2 seconden** vast voor het instellingenmenu. De afdrukbare dagkaart is de [Quick Reference](07%20Facilitator%20Quick%20Reference.md).

## What this game is

A roughly two-minute tablet driving game in which a student rides a scooter on a narrow Kenyan road. The student **tilts the tablet to steer**, presses the **right** side of the screen to go faster and the **left** side to brake. The road scrolls toward them; they control speed and direction. The goal is to drive safely (stay in the correct lane, do not speed, let people cross) and reach the **charge station** at the end of the route, overtaking slower vehicles cleanly along the way.

The game is not a driving simulator. It is a **career-impression experience**: a brief, physical taste of the decisions a rider makes under pressure on the road. The **debrief** after play is where the link to transport work and the career-exploration value is made.

**Students work together.** Each student adds their score to a shared group total, and a clean-driving streak carries across the whole team. Reaching the charge station and helping the team total is the goal, not the highest individual score.

## Session timing

| Phase | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Setup + pick a team name | 1 to 2 min | Facilitator opens and pins the game; the first student taps a team name |
| Each student's turn | about 2 min | Tilt to steer; drive safely; reach the charge station |
| Hand-off between students | about 30 sec | The charge-station screen shows the score and standings; pass the tablet; tap **Volgende speler** |
| Group of 4 students | about 10 min total | 4 turns + 3 hand-offs + setup |
| Group of 5 students | about 12 min total | one more turn + hand-off |
| Debrief | 3 to 5 min | a short facilitated conversation after everyone has played |

**Adjust for your rotation:** if the schedule is tight you can run 3 students in about 8 minutes, and the debrief can happen while the final score is on screen. The turn length and the hand-off wait time are both adjustable in the settings menu if you want a faster or slower rotation.

## Before the session (about 2 minutes)

- [ ] **Tablet charged**, ideally 50% or more for a 10-minute rotation. Playing while charging is fine and is the recommended state for an exhibit.
- [ ] **Open the game** by tapping its icon on the home screen.
- [ ] **Lock the tablet into the game (screen-pinning),** so children cannot drop out to the home screen or settings. Open the recent-apps view (the square button, or swipe up and hold), tap the game's icon at the top of its card, and choose **Pin / Vastzetten**. Now Home, Back and Recents keep the child inside the game. To unlock at the end of the day, hold **Back + Recents** together. (Full device setup is in the [tablet-setup checklist](03%20Tablet%20Setup%20and%20Golden%20Image%20Checklist.md).)
- [ ] **Landscape stays landscape.** The game locks its own orientation, so the screen will not flip during play.
- [ ] **Volume up** with the side buttons, and check the **brightness** is high enough for the room (turn off automatic brightness so it does not dim itself).
- [ ] **New class or new day?** Clear the old scores: open the settings menu (hold the **top-left corner 2 seconds**), then **BEHEER**, then **Klassement wissen** (tap, then tap again to confirm). If the same class is continuing, leave the scores.

> **How to open the settings menu (you need this for several steps)**
> There is no visible settings button, so children cannot reach it. **Press and hold the top-left corner of the screen for 2 seconds**; the game pauses and the menu opens. (On a computer, for testing, press **F8**.) Close it with **KLAAR** (bottom right). Everything you change is saved and stays saved, including after a restart.

### How to hold the tablet

The right hold is **two-handed landscape**, the tablet at about chest height, screen facing the student. The student tilts the whole tablet left and right to steer, like a steering wheel. Show the first student this hold; the rest will copy it from watching. The game can also be played in a holder or flat-ish in the hands; the tilt steering works either way.

### How to introduce the game to the class

Say this, or something like it, before the first student plays:

> *"This game puts you on a scooter on a road in Kenya. Your job is to drive safely through the traffic and reach the charging station at the end of your route. You steer by tilting the tablet. Everyone gets a turn, and your scores all add together. Ready?"*

That is all. You do not need to explain the controls in detail: when a turn starts, the game itself shows a short **"KANTEL OM TE STUREN"** (tilt to steer) hint with arrows, which clears the moment the student tilts. Letting students discover the controls is part of the experience (Gee, 2003). If a student is stuck, point at the on-screen hint: "tilt the tablet the way you want to go."

## During the session

### Starting each student's turn

1. On the start screen the prompt reads **"TIK EEN NAAM OM TE SPELEN"** (tap a name to play). The student (or you) **taps one of the team-name chips**; any name simply starts the game. You do not type a name. The names come from a fixed list and are kept unique on this tablet, so two groups never share one.
2. Hand the tablet to the student in **landscape**. The **"KANTEL OM TE STUREN"** hint appears for new players.
3. Step back. You do not need to coach during play.

### What you will see

- The student tilts to steer the scooter along the road; the world scrolls toward them automatically.
- **Safe driving scores:** a clean overtake, staying in the correct lane, and **letting a pedestrian cross** all earn points (a clean yield shows a green **"GOED GEWACHT!"**). Speeding, driving on the wrong side, and collisions lose points.
- **If the student crashes:** the screen briefly goes black-and-white, the world steps back a few seconds, and play continues. They lose some points but the turn does **not** end. This is deliberate: one crash should never end a child's turn. How forgiving this rewind is (0 to 3 times) is a setting you can change.
- After about two minutes, a **charge station** appears in the road ahead. When the student reaches it, the turn pauses automatically.

### At the charge-station hand-off

When a student reaches the charge station, the screen shows their turn score, the running **team total**, and the class standings (**KLASSEMENT**, with the current group highlighted). Then:

- Tap **Volgende speler** to pass the tablet straight into the next teammate's turn.
- The last player of the group sees the **eindstand** (final standings).
- For the next group, tap **Nieuwe groep**.

There is no rush; the screen waits. If you want a fixed pause before it advances on its own, that wait time is a setting (**SPEELDUUR**).

### What not to do

- **Do not over-explain the controls.** The game shows the tilt hint itself; let students discover the rest. Discovery produces better engagement and retention (Gee, 2003).
- **Do not intervene during a crash.** The rewind handles it. If a student freezes during the black-and-white flash, reassure them that it continues on its own.
- **Do not compare individual scores during play.** The team total is what matters; save comparisons for the debrief.
- **Do not rush the hand-off.** Use the pause for a calm physical transfer.

## Adjusting the game between groups (optional)

Open the settings menu (hold the **top-left corner 2 seconds**) to tune how the game plays. Everything you change is saved for the next group. The most useful dials:

- **MOEILIJKHEID** and **VERKEER EN WEG**: how many hazards, how much traffic, how strict the rules, how many pedestrians. Lower these for younger or first-time groups.
- **SNELHEID EN GEVOEL**: how fast and how lively the scooter feels.
- **BESTURING**: tilt sensitivity, and **bewegingsgevoeligheid** (lower it for a child who feels motion-sick).
- **OMGEVING**: time of day, and the **rijden links/rechts** switch (drive on the **left**, the Kenyan rule, or the **right**, the Dutch rule, a nice teaching lever).
- **GELUID**: master, music and ambient volume, and vibration on/off.
- **SPEELDUUR**: how long a turn lasts and the hand-off wait time.

Two facilitator **actions** also live here:

- **BESTURING > KALIBREER** re-zeroes the tilt steering if it has drifted (hold the tablet the way the player will hold it, then tap).
- **BEHEER > Klassement wissen** clears the leaderboard for a new class (confirm twice).

To undo everything at once, tap **Alles terug naar standaard** (bottom left). A fuller description of every setting is in [FACILITATOR: Changing the Game (Dutch settings guide)](../De%20Ontdekfabriek%20Internship/FACILITATOR%20%E2%80%94%20Changing%20the%20Game.md), and the line between what you can change here and what needs a developer is in [What You Can Change vs What Needs a Developer](04%20What%20You%20Can%20Change%20vs%20What%20Needs%20a%20Developer.md).

## After the session: the debrief

The debrief is where the career-exploration value lives. The game provides the concrete experience (Kolb, 1984); the debrief turns it into reflection. Aim for 3 to 5 minutes.

### Opening, personal experience first
> "What was it like? What did you do first?"

Let students describe what happened before you analyse it; get them talking in their own words.

### Linking to the work
> "Driving through traffic like this is the kind of thing people do in transport work. What decisions did you have to make?"

Expected answers: when to overtake, whether to speed up, how close to get to oncoming traffic, when to let someone cross. Name them: "those are the kinds of calls someone working in transport makes every day."

### The group-score question
> "Your scores all added together. If one person crashed and lost points, what did that mean for the group?"

This surfaces the interdependence the relay is built on: transport work is collaborative, and one person's decisions affect the whole route.

### The Kenya connection
> "The game is set in Kenya. Did anything look different from what you expected?"

Let students notice the road colour, the vehicles, the signs, the side of the road they drove on. If the Swahili time labels came up, name one: "ASUBUHI means morning in Swahili."

### Optional, the career
> "Would you want to do something like this as work? What surprised you about it?"

This is the TATOE question: not "should you choose this career" but "what was surprising or different from what you assumed?"

## Troubleshooting

Most fixes start by opening the settings menu: **hold the top-left corner for 2 seconds**. This is the same list as the laminated [Quick Reference card](07%20Facilitator%20Quick%20Reference.md), in fuller form.

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Steering feels crooked or off-centre** | Settings > **BESTURING** > **KALIBREER**. Hold the tablet the way the player should hold it, then tap. This re-zeroes the tilt (sensor drift is the most common real complaint, and this fixes it). |
| **Steering does not respond at all** | The tablet must not be lying flat, and its **motion sensor** must be on in Android settings. Close and reopen the app if it persists. |
| **Left and right feel reversed** | Settings > **BESTURING** > turn **Sturen omkeren** on. |
| **A child feels dizzy or motion-sick** | Settings > **BESTURING** > lower **Bewegingsgevoeligheid**. |
| **The game froze or is not responding** | Wait about 10 seconds (it can pause on a transition). Still stuck: close and reopen the app. Still stuck: restart the tablet. **Scores are saved and will not be lost.** |
| **No sound** | Turn the tablet volume up with the side buttons, then Settings > **GELUID** and raise **Hoofdvolume**, **Muziekvolume** or **Omgevingsgeluid**. |
| **The screen is too dark** | Raise the tablet brightness (swipe down from the top) and turn off automatic brightness so it does not dim itself. |
| **Too easy, or too hard** | Settings > **MOEILIJKHEID** and **VERKEER EN WEG** (cars, pedestrians, how strict). |
| **The game settings got messed up** | Settings > **Alles terug naar standaard** (bottom left) puts everything back to how it shipped. |
| **Scores need clearing for a new class** | Settings > **BEHEER** > **Klassement wissen** (tap twice to confirm). This cannot be undone, so only between classes. |
| **The screen is the wrong way round** | The game is locked to landscape. If it still looks wrong, close and reopen the app. |
| **The Home/Back buttons do nothing, you cannot leave the game** | This is on purpose: the game is **pinned**. To leave, hold **Back + Recents** together (enter the tablet unlock code if asked). Pin it again afterwards (see Before the session). |
| **A child does not know how to steer** | The **"KANTEL OM TE STUREN"** hint shows by itself. Tell them: "tilt the tablet like a steering wheel, the way you want to go." |

### If nothing else works
Restart the tablet (hold power, then Restart). The game opens to the start screen. The leaderboard and your settings are saved, so nothing is lost. Pin the game again before the next group.

## Privacy note for facilitators

The game stores **no personal data** and uses **no internet**. The leaderboard holds a **team name chosen from a fixed list** (not the child's own name) and the team's total, saved only on this tablet, and you can wipe it any time (**BEHEER > Klassement wissen**). There is no camera, microphone, login or account. If a parent or teacher asks, you can say: *"The game doesn't record anything about your child. No camera, no microphone, no names, no internet. It only keeps the team's score on this tablet, and we can wipe that any time."* The full statement is in [Privacy (AVG/GDPR) and Accessibility Note](05%20Privacy%20AVG%20GDPR%20and%20Accessibility%20Note.md).
